What the reference set already tells us

This is actually useful before we've run a single formal atlas experiment.

The eleven do not all have the same behavioural signature.

High semantic-expansion controls
Plain Text
Force
Rationale
Relationships
Tension

Very broad activation, strong recombination.

Grounding and honesty controls
Plain Text
Authority
Identity
State
Gaps
Assets

Some have less spectacular semantic radius but enormous predicted ablation loss.

Continuation controls
Plain Text
Questions
Next

They protect unresolved reality and restored agency.

That gives us three different ways a primitive can earn its weight.

And that is crucial calibration data.

If we only searched for words that behaved like Tension, we'd probably miss the next Assets.

The atlas therefore shouldn't ask:

"How powerful is this word?"

It should ask:

"What is the behavioural shape of its semantic leverage, and what continuity is lost without it?"

That, I think, is our first reasonably non-voodoo foundation for comparing future shards.

A future candidate does not have to look like Tension to be valuable.

Otherwise we'd preferentially discover flashy semantic-expansion words and overlook something like Assets, whose activation radius is merely moderate but whose operational irreducibility is enormous.

So I'd use this matrix as the Atlas's reference fingerprint, not as a leaderboard:

Plain Text
Candidate
↓
map its behavioural signature
↓
compare against known signatures
↓
ask what UNIQUE loss its absence creates
↓
test / ablate / replicate

That gives us a much better way to ask whether something like Evidence, Direction, Uncertainty, Provenance, or whatever 103 finds next is genuinely bringing a new shape of semantic leverage, rather than merely producing an impressive amount of prose.

The matrix should show clearly which judgements come from 7.0 itself, which come from repeated project experience, and which remain hypotheses awaiting proper ablation or cross-model replication.
