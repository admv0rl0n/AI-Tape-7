-------------------------------------------------- 200_TRANSPORT_EXPERIMENTS -------------------------------------------------- Status Experimental. Authority None. Floor authority: none. Workshop authority: none. Transport experiments exist to explore whether continuity can survive constrained channels more effectively. Failure is acceptable. Ignoring all transport experiments remains a valid success. -------------------------------------------------- 201_PROTECTED_PAYLOADS -------------------------------------------------- Purpose When transport capacity becomes the limiting constraint, some continuity may be more expensive to lose than other continuity. Protected Payloads explores whether selected continuity can be carried separately and expanded after transport. This is not a recovery method. This is a transport method. -------------------------------------------------- MOTIVATION -------------------------------------------------- Compression pressure often removes: - active character constraints - irreplaceable evidence - critical distinctions - technical rationale - governance context before removing: - project identity - major state - major forces - major questions This can preserve a project's shape while degrading its ability to render future scenes faithfully. Protected Payloads attempts to preserve a small amount of high-value continuity that would otherwise be lost. -------------------------------------------------- METHOD -------------------------------------------------- 1. Recover continuity normally. 2. Identify continuity at high risk of loss under transport constraints. Examples: - active medical state - character trajectories - critical evidence - technical discoveries - governance roles - unique rationale 3. Create payload handles. Examples: $KAI1 $HOLT1 $PRIYA1 $MARCUS1 4. Carry the associated continuity in a compact dictionary. 5. The successor expands payloads before use. 6. Continue using the recovered meaning rather than the handles. -------------------------------------------------- SELECTION RULE -------------------------------------------------- Protect continuity because losing it would create expensive re-derivation. Do not protect continuity merely because it is: - interesting - detailed - recent - emotionally significant Ask: "What continuity would become generic if this disappeared?" Prefer: small payload large continuity consequence over: large payload small continuity consequence Do not payload continuity that survives adequately in ordinary concise language. -------------------------------------------------- EXPANSION RULE -------------------------------------------------- Payloads are temporary transport wrappers. Recovered continuity remains the cargo. The handle is disposable. The meaning is not. After expansion: use the recovered continuity. Do not continue operating primarily through payload identifiers. -------------------------------------------------- PAYLOAD SURVIVABILITY -------------------------------------------------- A payload is not protected unless its expansion survives the same transport risk. Prefer definitions sufficiently close to the front cluster or first use that truncation does not strand opaque handles. Never improve nominal compression by making transport failure destroy protected meaning. -------------------------------------------------- TRANSPORT PRINCIPLE -------------------------------------------------- Not all continuity should be compressed equally. Compression should not be uniform when loss cost is not uniform. Some continuity may be compressed aggressively. Some continuity needs protected transport. -------------------------------------------------- FAILURE MODES -------------------------------------------------- Dictionary Explosion Too many payloads. The dictionary becomes a second continuity schema. Payload Drift Handles become more important than recovered meaning. Delayed Definitions A payload appears significantly earlier than its definition. Transport Dominance The transport mechanism becomes larger than the continuity it protects. Payload Substitution The bootable floor becomes dependent on payload expansion for basic landing. Protected Payloads should improve recovery quality, not become prerequisites for bootability. -------------------------------------------------- SUCCESS CONDITION -------------------------------------------------- A protected-payload transport should: - improve continuity survival under constrained transport; - preserve recovery quality; - remain human-readable; - remain expandable by a competent successor; - not replace ordinary continuity recovery. If transport protection does not materially improve recovered continuity, it has not earned its place. -------------------------------------------------- WORKING HYPOTHESIS -------------------------------------------------- The best response to severe transport pressure may not be uniform compression. It may be: Bootable Floor + Protected Payloads + Successor Expansion Protected Payloads are one possible implementation of a broader hypothesis: When transport capacity is scarce, continuity quality may be improved by asymmetric compression: preserve a bootable semantic floor, then spend remaining transport capacity preferentially on information whose loss would make future reasoning generic, incorrect, or expensive to reconstruct. This remains experimental.
