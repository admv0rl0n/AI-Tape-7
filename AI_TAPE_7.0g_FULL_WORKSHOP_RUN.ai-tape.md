# 08_RATIONALE

## Why 7.0 remains stable

7.0 provides a small, consistent, bootable recovery substrate. Although some scaffold-rich ancestors sometimes produced richer tapes, 7.0 is a cleaner baseline and a useful control against which experimental methods can be measured.

## Why the Workshop is separate

The project repeatedly learned that useful techniques can become burdensome when promoted into travelling floor machinery. Workshop therefore contains techniques; floor contains dimensions. Repeated use alone does not grant authority.

## Why Calibration was back-ported

Clean-room/8.0 work surfaced a real distinction between missing information and misoriented interpretation. Rather than add Bearings as a twelfth dimension, the floor received a tiny general calibration question: what grounds understanding, what remains uncertain, and what would materially change it.

## Why anti-chainsaw wording was back-ported

Years of testing showed that models can over-compress, truncate or flatten continuity while still producing apparently clean artifacts. The concise rebase boundary protects materially important distinctions without introducing a rich-output subsystem.

## Why 8.0 exists separately

8.0 tests a different proposition: recovery may improve if the semantic projection is chosen for the inherited reality and continuation purpose before reconstruction. Folding Projection into 7.0 would destroy the fixed-map control and muddy the experiment.

## Why lower-tier “Deck” research occurred

Once testing suggested Tape quality depends heavily on model capability, the project considered whether cognition could be externalised into a deeper procedural tool for lower-capability models. AI DECK represented that hypothesis. Its first implementation failed to be understood/executed by the target low-tier models, so it currently stands as negative evidence rather than a deployment path.

## Why the Semantic Leverage Lab persists

Small words/phrases such as Palimpsest, Trace, Survives, Despite, Bearings and anti-compression statements have repeatedly appeared capable of shifting what models notice or recover. The Lab investigates whether those effects are reproducible, distinct, domain-dependent, functionally valuable or merely literary/procedural artefacts.

# 101_DEEP_RECOVERY_GUIDANCE

## 101A_HUNT_SCARS

### Scar: Recorder was treated as causal

Historical symptom:
Recorder/role machinery became increasingly elaborate as the project tried to force fuller, richer handoffs.

Recovered lesson:
Changing/removing Recorder language did not eliminate continuity. Recorder problems were likely symptomatic of deeper generation/capability/runtime pressures rather than caused by a privileged Recorder concept.

Current effect:
Avoid rebuilding recorder machinery merely because output is under-dense.

### Scar: Micro-Tape duplicated the low path

Historical symptom:
Micro was pushed as an ultra-small orientation mechanism.

Recovered lesson:
Its main value was experimental. It helped expose that existing shallow recovery was already sufficient for orientation, while an additional named low path created confusion.

Current effect:
Do not preserve experimental mechanisms merely because they taught something valuable.

### Scar: Granite supplied external cognition and counterpressure

Historical symptom:
Large specification machinery accumulated around recurring continuity failure modes.

Recovered lesson:
Some machinery was useful not because it described fundamental continuity, but because it constrained or compensated for model behaviour.

Current effect:
Distinguish fundamental continuity need from compensating scaffold.

### Scar: Chainsaw minimalism

Historical symptom:
Review pressure toward smaller, cleaner artifacts sometimes threatened useful depth/history/context.

Recovered lesson:
Protocol smallness and recovery smallness are different. Compression that destroys judgement-relevant distinctions exports reconstruction cost to Darren/successor.

Current effect:
Keep the floor small without treating thin handoffs as inherently superior.

### Scar: File identity gremlins

Historical symptom:
Operational cards, MAX/replay artifacts and generated packs were confused under incorrect labels.

Recovered lesson:
Transport/file identity errors can masquerade as protocol problems.

Current effect:
Verify actual artifact identity before architectural diagnosis.

### Scar: AI DECK low-tier failure

Historical symptom:
A detailed serial workbook was designed so lower-capability models could execute bounded continuity steps.

Observed report:
Target low-tier models did not understand or execute it.

Recovered lesson:
More explicit procedure does not automatically substitute for the capability needed even to follow a continuity appliance.

Current effect:
Do not assume lower-tier continuity can be solved by instruction depth alone.
